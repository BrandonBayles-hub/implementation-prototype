Why we still buy seven numbers
Today, when a contract is signed, the system automatically buys seven phone numbers for the property — one for each way we might reach a resident. ELI 2.0 replaces all of that with a single Super Agent number, so a 2.0 property only needs one. We are not changing the purchase yet, for three reasons:
1. We are mid-rollout. Some properties going live right now are still on 1.0 and genuinely need all seven.
2. Deciding per property means a person. Someone would have to open each contract and judge how many numbers to buy. That is slow, and it will be wrong often enough to matter.
3. Buying a number later is the expensive mistake. An unused number is a small monthly fee. A number bought after the fact has to be registered with the carrier and approved on the messaging campaign before it can send anything — days of waiting, and the exact failure that caused the Sweeny Clan number swap.
So we over-buy on purpose and let Activate pick which of the seven becomes the Super Agent number. When product sets a date, the purchase drops to one. That is a calendar decision, not something to wait on until 2.0 is finished.
The flag cannot live in OXP — one stop shop, outside the client environment
The flag is what puts a property on the Go Live list. It has been proposed that we set it inside OXP, at the client and property level. That does not work.
OXP is the client's environment. Setting the flag there means an Entrata employee has to go into each client's own environment to do it. That access is restricted — just-in-time (JIT) approval, and not always granted. Every property we migrate would start with an access request. That is a blocker, not a process.
Doing this over and over is a bad solution. Walking client by client, property by property, and flipping the same switch is the expensive version of this work. The right shape is one stop shop: one internal tool that can flag globally — across all clients and all properties — from a single screen.
Recommendation: Client Admin, Amplitude, or an equivalent internal tool — not the client environment. Those sit outside OXP. An internal team can turn 2.0 on for a wave of clients without logging into each one. Once everyone is on 2.0, the flag goes away; do not build this into self-service or into the product the client uses.
To be explicit: Go Live is self-service and stays. Flagging a property as 2.0 is not self-service — it is internal, temporary, and belongs in one global place outside the client environment. Do not solve them in the same spot.
